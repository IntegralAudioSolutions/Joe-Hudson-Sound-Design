// Handing Off To Implementation

Not every credit ends with the sound designer placing the final mix themselves. On work like this, part of the job was designing the palette of UI sounds and then handing them over to the technical sound designer building them into the engine — with instructions on intent and timing rather than a finished implementation to copy exactly.

A previz like the one above is that handoff document. It's a rough pass showing roughly where and when each sound should land, built so the person doing the implementation understands the intent behind the design rather than just inheriting a folder of files and guessing.

That handoff is often the part of the job that matters most. A sound can be designed perfectly and still land badly if the person implementing it doesn't understand why it was built that way. Communicating the intent clearly — what should trigger it, roughly when, how it should feel against everything else happening on screen — is frequently more critical to the end result than the sound design itself.

// Dialogue Editing

One of my first gigs in the industry, and one I still take a quiet pride in. Paragon shipped a huge amount of hero dialogue, and my job was cutting, rendering and labelling the raw voiceover recordings ready for implementation — catchphrases, confirm selections, ability barks, interactions and team comms — across heroes including Sevarog, Grux, GRIM.exe, Feng Mao, Murdock, TwinBlast, Gideon, Howitzer, Steel and Sparrow. On my best day I got through over 1,000 lines, cut, rendered and labelled with zero mistakes, inside a single eight-hour session.

The workflow ran one audio track per session, with the VO artist recording somewhere between one and ten attempts at each line. I'd identify the best take, edit it down and rename the region directly in Reaper, then run a final check pass against the script before the file names went through a second, automated pass in Advanced Renamer to bring everything in line with the naming convention. Finished files were foldered off by type — catchphrases, confirm selections, interactions, taunts, team comms and so on — then zipped and sent back with the relevant Excel script for that character, line references and all.
